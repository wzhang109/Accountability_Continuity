# Accountability Continuity

**Wenwen (Celine) Zhang · Independent research in progress**

## Why I started

As AI takes on more tasks we once thought only people could do, **what does it mean to be human, and what do we value about being human?** For me, human flourishing includes having room to think, to learn, and to decide what matters to us. My [early philosophical essay](the-gap-that-cant-close.md) and [reflection on purpose and judgment](docs/purpose-judgment-and-accountability.md) explore why those things matter even when a machine can do a task well.

When I was studying economics at the University of San Francisco, I was cautiously optimistic about what AI might help people do. I hadn’t expected its capabilities to change so quickly. Over time, I found myself thinking about a student who finishes an assignment faster with AI. The time saved is real. If the purpose of the assignment was to learn how to solve the next problem, though, I’d also want to know what the student had a chance to work out for themselves.

At the University of Chicago, I encountered a version of that question in my own work. I checked AI-generated industry codes against official definitions. To decide whether a code fit, I had to compare what a firm actually made with what the category covered. Working through a close call helped me see what to look for in the next case. That experience gave my research a starting point. **Under what conditions does working with AI help people develop their own judgment?**

## Where I am looking now

I study how teams divide work when AI drafts a recommendation and a person must decide what happens next. Who decides what a good outcome is? Does the person responsible have the evidence, time, and authority to act?

Imagine a team building a dataset. Its goal is to produce reliable classifications, but its workflow requires a code for every entry. When a source is ambiguous, even a careful reviewer has to guess. Letting that reviewer mark a case as uncertain would give them a way to serve the original goal. This is an example of a design question, not a finding about a particular team.

## The first comparison

**Does putting evidence beside an AI recommendation make it easier to check, or just easier to believe?** In the proposed study, both groups review the same kinds of fictional cases. One sees the relevant passage immediately; the other can reveal the identical passage with one click. I would count both errors caught and correct recommendations sent back unnecessarily. Sending more cases back is not necessarily better judgment.

The cases, allocation code, and synthetic analysis checks are in place. No participants have taken part. The comparison concerns decisions under a given rule; it cannot tell us how a team should choose the rule, or whether someone is thinking independently.

I’ve also written about [when an extra review step might help, and what a review tool could look like](docs/positive-friction-and-product-directions.md). That’s a separate idea from the first comparison.

## From proposal to pilot

On November 15, 2026, Foresight Institute invited me to pitch *Easier to Check or Easier to Believe? Putting Evidence Beside AI Recommendations* at its Vision Weekend Idea Grant Competition. I’m exploring small grants and fellowships to run the pilot. I’d also like to hear from people who review AI-drafted work. When you flag a mistake, who can change the decision? If a colleague catches something you missed, how do you find out?

## Other ways to explore the work

- **Measurement.** The [model walkthrough, in English and Chinese](simulations/override_identification_v2/PLAIN_LANGUAGE_GUIDE.md) shows why the same rate of human overrides can arise from more accurate AI or less human checking. This is a mathematical example under stated assumptions.
- **Simulation checks.** The [November 9 audit](simulations/override_identification_v2/VERIFICATION_2026-11-09.md) records the model checks, corrected fictional assignment, and matched-sample numerical comparisons. It separates statistical simulation from preset-response software tests.
- **Organizations.** The [reading notes](readings/README.md) follow how work passes between people. The [pre-triage reading](readings/before-the-clock-starts-2026-11-17.md) uses observed patient journeys to examine which parts of a handoff a timestamp can show. The [English Wikipedia patrol case](readings/ip-access-and-patrol-work-2026-11-15.md) examines how role-based IP access changes the route for investigating edits and what closure time can show about the work. One [public review record](readings/review-to-implementation-2026-11-07.md) links a request to a reply and a change in a released artifact. It does not show whether the change helped users. A [non-adoption contrast](readings/non-adoption-and-criteria-2026-11-09.md) separates an explicit refusal from an unreceived request, with readiness and comprehension limits. The [public-document pilot](studies/organizational-feedback/README.md) traces other reported issues and responses. A [scoped-outcome reading](readings/recovery-and-outcome-scope-2026-11-11.md) keeps execution, restoration and later prevention separate. A [routine editor case](readings/position-and-shortcut-2026-11-13.md) shows why verification belongs to a specific task, route and version.
- **Learning.** A [separate three-group proposal](studies/ai-advice-timing/DRAFT_PROTOCOL.md) asks whether seeing AI advice before or after forming an initial judgment affects later performance without AI. The [youth design](studies/youth-advice-workflow/README.md) is a further proposal. Neither study has participant results.
- **How the ideas developed.** The [research log](research-log.md) records changes in thinking. The [early index proposal](identification-strategy.md) is preserved as a historical design. It is not a validated measure of responsibility.

My background is in development economics and public policy. The questions and research motivation are mine; AI tools contributed substantially to design, code, and writing. I welcome feedback on whether the proposed comparisons can answer the questions they pose.
