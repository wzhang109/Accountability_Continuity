# Advice-timing design versions and the next research step

**Working audit · October 6, 2026 · Wenwen (Celine) Zhang**

The project now has a general youth proposal, a more detailed adult operational
draft, and a recently submitted adolescent research-grant proposal. These are
related design documents, not three completed studies. This note makes their
differences explicit before further materials are developed.

The grant application was submitted on October 6. Submission is not funding,
institutional approval, recruitment, or an implemented study. There are still
no adolescent participants or original learning outcomes. This audit was
prepared with substantial AI assistance; it is not independent methodological
validation.

## Which design answers which question?

| Design choice | Existing adult operational draft | Submitted adolescent feasibility proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Population | Adults, as a proposed initial feasibility option | A proposed first age band of 15–17, within a broader interest in ages 13–17 |
| Starting sample | Feasibility before any justified confirmatory sample size | Up to 12 pretest participants and 90 different randomized participants, 30 per arm, as planning targets |
| Initial session | 9 baseline, 24 practice, and 12 immediate no-AI items | 6 baseline and 12 practice items, provisionally 45–60 minutes |
| Delayed assessment | 12 no-AI items, target seven days | 8 new related no-AI items, approximately seven days later |
| Later advice challenge | A common advice-first interface for all three original arms | 4 further advice items for the assisted groups; the comparison group continues without advice |
| Confidence and reasons | Initial measures in judgment first; final measures shared across arms | An initial answer, confidence, and brief reason before advice in judgment first; comparable answer/reason and review opportunities in the other workflows |
| Oversight | Appropriate partner review and consent before any recruitment | An adolescent-research partner, ethics review, parental consent and youth assent before recruitment |

The counts are draft specifications, not administered item banks or established
power calculations. These designs must not be merged silently. In particular,
the adolescent challenge cannot be described as a common-current-interface
comparison of all three arms. Any contrast with the no-AI group there combines
prior workflow and current assistance.

The submitted proposal has no separate immediate unaided assessment. Its
current or practice performance is different from an immediate test completed
without advice. If a later protocol adds that assessment, it should identify
the change and account for the added testing and participant burden.

## What the primary comparison can establish

The proposed primary outcome is accuracy on new related tasks without advice
at the delayed assessment. The primary comparison is judgment first versus
advice first. The no-AI arm is a separately reported benchmark.

That comparison concerns the assigned workflow package. Recording an initial
answer adds effort, commitment and a point of reference as well as changing
when advice appears. It does not identify a pure timing effect. A correct final
answer during assisted practice, disagreement with advice, or a completed
initial response is not itself evidence of independently understood reasoning.

Confidence should be compared with accuracy on the same answer at the same
stage. Explanation quality requires a reviewed scoring rubric and raters who
do not know the assigned arm. Task confidence and exploratory questions about
ownership must not be presented as validated measures of general agency,
mental health, or enduring independence of thought.

## The next useful deliverables

1. A clearly labeled youth feasibility protocol consistent with the submitted
   proposal, preserving the adult draft as a separate design option.
2. A small, internally reviewed set of fictional school-project tasks,
   explicit answer keys, and matched correct and misleading advice. AI review
   is not a substitute for educator or adolescent-specialist review.
3. A measure-to-claim table identifying which responses can support delayed
   task performance, appropriate advice use, explanation quality and burden.
4. A versioned analysis outline and data dictionary suitable for discussion
   with a statistician or institutional partner. No fabricated participant
   records will be treated as pilot evidence.

The practical decision is whether asking a learner for an initial judgment
adds enough learning value to justify its effort. The materials should make
that question testable and useful, while allowing a benefit, a cost or an
inconclusive result.

## Relationship to the organizational branch

The organizational public-document pilot studies a different object: what
reported records establish about an issue, its treatment, and later assessment.
It does not estimate the effect of the learning workflow above. Its next step
is to seek a traceable issue-response record and independently review the
coding, rather than infer institutional learning from one corrected output.

Both branches are motivated by the same concern about judgment, opportunities
to learn and the purposes of AI use. Their populations, measures and evidence
remain separate.
