# Anticipated teaching and evidence of learning

**Bounded reading · October 23, 2026 · AI assistance disclosed**

Kellogg and colleagues' *Novice risk work* (2025,
[author-hosted published article](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Novice-risk-work--How-juniors-coaching-seniors-on-emerging_2025_Information-_390902d0-017e-4111-9824-71e4eab5bef0.pdf),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infoandorg.2025.100559)) examines interviews
after a BCG GPT-4 task. The focal phase has 78 interviews; Appendix A also
describes an earlier 118-interview phase. These counts are not an effect-study
sample of observed teaching pairs (printed pp. 6, 15–16).

The authors compare proposed tactics with expert literature. They explicitly
lack managers' actual reactions and risk practices tried in everyday work;
company policy then prohibited that use (p. 13). Accordingly, the article's
learning-failure argument is an interpretation of anticipated practices,
not a measured effect of junior-to-senior instruction.

One source-timing issue remains open: Appendix A states a before-July-2023
expert-literature cutoff, while Table D1 cites Shavit et al. (2023), whose
[publisher announcement](https://openai.com/index/practices-for-governing-agentic-ai-systems/) is dated December 14, 2023
(pp. 15, 17, 21). The precise corpus and versions need reconciliation;
this note does not infer misconduct or overturn the findings.

## Questions this raises

These are questions generated by the reading, not adopted hypotheses:

- When does familiarity with a tool become a reason to trust someone as a
  teacher? What would establish the relevant knowledge in a particular task?
- If advice is technically sound, can the person receiving it obtain the
  time, authority and support needed to act on it? Who would need to take part?
- What do the people involved want to preserve or change through teaching?
  Where might a satisfactory result for one person leave another dissatisfied?

To investigate one question, distinguish the advice offered, an actual
interaction, the recipient's response and a later outcome. A prospective
observation could help describe a process; estimating a teaching effect would
also require a defensible comparison and appropriate outcomes. No such records
have been collected here. Advice outside an employee's formal role may reflect
both limited knowledge and limited authority; this reading cannot separate them.

## Boundaries and next reading

Beane's [*Shadow Learning*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0001839217751692) remains an abstract-only reading.
Its 2017 [MIT dissertation record](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/b888f508-d6c2-4324-818e-45211402158a) was accessible, but the
PDF download failed. No detailed surgical episode is coded from that abstract,
and no claim is transferred from robot-assisted surgery to generative AI.

Next, seek an accessible original study of an actual teaching interaction,
including a case that challenges an emerging interpretation. Keep questions
about educational purpose and relationships open. This is not a systematic
review, a novelty claim, or a validated theory. Researcher and independent
human review remain pending.
