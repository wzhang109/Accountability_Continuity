# Focused primary-source positioning

**Checked October 7, 2026 · targeted reading, not a systematic review**

These studies motivate measuring learning separately from assisted performance.
They do not establish the outcome of this proposed adolescent workflow.

| Primary source | Verified connection and limits |
|---|---|
| [Bastani et al 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/) | High-school mathematics study separates assisted practice and unaided exam performance, with different tutor designs. It supports separate outcomes; it does not show this script-based sequence helps. Its same-session assessment is different from the proposed one-week assessment. |
| [Gajos and Mamykina 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05402) | In adult nutrition tasks, improved assisted decisions did not automatically produce learning. Their judgment-before-recommendation condition did not itself establish learning gains. This cautions against assuming that recording a first answer is sufficient. |
| [Buçinca et al 2021](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml) | Cognitive forcing reduced overreliance in the studied decision task but introduced usability and motivation trade-offs. Its result is not adolescent delayed learning evidence; burden belongs in this pilot. |
| [Shen and Tamkin 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills) | Coding study distinguishes task performance from comprehension. Associations between interaction styles and learning are not randomized style effects, and short follow-up cannot establish lasting skill formation. Adult coding is different from school-record reasoning. |
| [Yan 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1936522/full) | Published September 28. The current primary-source search extract confirms a three-arm preservice-teacher scenario study comparing judgment-first and advice-first; the manipulation includes commitment and review procedure. This is a close precedent, so the three-arm design is not claimed as new. |

## October 10 baseline development check

Re-read the [Gajos and Mamykina primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05402)
on October 10. Its distinction between assisted decisions and learning motivates
keeping starting and later unaided measures separate; it does not validate these
school-record items. No new effect estimate or novelty claim is derived here.
The PMC Bastani page returned a browser challenge today, so its full methods were
not freshly reviewed. The earlier source notes remain dated as such.

The six new baseline records are invented materials, not copied instruments or
extracted observations. Their answer bases follow explicitly supplied counts and
rules; B04 includes two compatible denominator scenarios with opposite rankings,
and B06 leaves the cause unidentified by the supplied record. Semantic, age and
measurement judgments require human review; arithmetic checks cannot provide it.
The feedback-reactivity and unequal-form issues in BASELINE_REVIEW.md are design
risks to examine, not empirical findings about these tasks.

## October 12 practice-family source check

Re-read the authors' [Gajos and Mamykina abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05402):
the studied initial-choice condition improved assisted decisions without
establishing learning gains. Re-read the authors' [Buçinca et al abstract](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml):
reduced overreliance came with less favorable subjective ratings and differing
benefits by motivation. These abstracts motivate separate learning and burden
measures; they do not validate these new records or prescribe their wording.
This is a focused source refresh, not a new systematic literature search.

The rate examples and counterexamples are invented, not adapted published
instruments. Answer balance and simple computations are development decisions;
they are not a demonstrated novelty, psychological mechanism or validated scale.
