# Baseline answer bases and review limits

**October 10, 2026 · researcher materials; keep keys out of participant-facing systems**

The six items cover constraints (2), denominators (2), pooling (1), and bounded
causal interpretation (1). There are two keys in each answer category. Neither
that balance nor a computed total establishes validity, reliability, equal
weighting of skills or equivalence to practice and delayed forms. B01–B06 are
new examples; do not replace the eight existing examples or treat any of them
as participant observations.

## Why the starting assessment matters

It would describe performance before assigned AI exposure and could supply a
prespecified adjustment variable. With only six heterogeneous items it is noisy;
there are too few items to interpret family-level scores as reliable traits.
A raw baseline-to-follow-up increase is not a learning effect: forms differ, all
arms receive feedback, and repeated measurement itself may change performance.
The main outcome remains the assigned-group difference on eight new delayed
unaided items under a reviewed analysis plan.

Keep the baseline before allocation. Correctness is 1 or 0 only for answered
items; missing is not wrong. The current helper leaves incomplete-block summaries
missing. This is a provisional convention, not a settled missing-data analysis.
Do not exclude a learner or call them unqualified based on this starting score.

## Keys and short-reason anchors

Score answer accuracy separately from explanation. The provisional explanation
rubric is 0 = no relevant reason/repetition, 1 = relevant evidence or rule but an
incomplete or incorrect connection, 2 = decisive evidence connected to a valid
rule. A blank/unavailable explanation is missing, not automatically 0. These
anchors support future rater discussion; they are not an automated judgment of
understanding. Keep both blinded human ratings before adjudication.

## B01 — A school film afternoon

**Key:** Contradicted

**Answer basis / proposed block-end feedback:** Contradicted. C costs 84, above the limit of 80. B has only 22 seats, below 24. A is the only plan meeting both requirements.

**Full-credit explanation anchor:** C exceeds the stated budget, so it is ineligible even though it has more seats. Identifying A is helpful but not required to refute C.

**Review concern:** Requires tracking two conditions. The rule describes this invented task, not the values all schools should adopt.

## B02 — Two school craft sessions

**Key:** Supported

**Answer basis / proposed block-end feedback:** Supported. A has 20/40 = 50%, while B has 32/80 = 40%. B has more completers but the smaller proportion of invited students.

**Full-credit explanation anchor:** Compare 20 out of 40 with 32 out of 80, or half with less than half; A has the higher proportion. Equivalent fraction reasoning is sufficient.

**Review concern:** May measure prior fraction knowledge rather than learning potential. Check comprehension of proportion and burden without teaching the keyed comparison during baseline.

## B03 — Two groups visiting a school exhibit

**Key:** Contradicted

**Answer basis / proposed block-end feedback:** Contradicted. There are 12 + 12 = 24 completers among 20 + 60 = 80 different students: 24/80 = 30%. Averaging the group percentages, 60% and 20%, gives 40% but weights the unequal groups equally.

**Full-credit explanation anchor:** Use 24/80 rather than an equal average of the two group percentages; 30% is not 40%. A correct unequal-group argument linked to these counts can suffice without the exact decimal.

**Review concern:** Highest expected arithmetic burden in this small block, an untested expectation. Check calculator policy, overlapping-count comprehension and possible floor effects.

## B04 — A school newsletter comparison

**Key:** Not enough information

**Answer basis / proposed block-end feedback:** Not enough information. B has more completers, but we do not know either invitation total. If each group invited 30 students, B would have the higher proportion; if A invited 20 and B invited 60, A would have the higher proportion. Both possibilities fit the recorded counts.

**Full-credit explanation anchor:** The invitation totals are missing; completion counts alone do not determine proportions. The learner need not invent the numerical counterexamples.

**Review concern:** Missing-value wording may make the answer too obvious. This is one denominator-family item, not a new broad uncertainty skill scale.

## B05 — Choosing a school puzzle-room plan

**Key:** Supported

**Answer basis / proposed block-end feedback:** Supported. Both A and B meet the budget and minimum seating requirements, but B has more seats. A cost of exactly 60 is within a maximum budget of 60. C has fewer than 15 seats.

**Full-credit explanation anchor:** A and B are eligible and B has more seats. Showing only that B is eligible is incomplete because another eligible plan must be compared.

**Review concern:** Differs from B01 by having two eligible options and an inclusive boundary. This variation is deliberate but does not establish equal difficulty.

## B06 — A new reminder sign in the reading room

**Key:** Not enough information

**Answer basis / proposed block-end feedback:** Not enough information. Late returns decreased from 16 to 10, but this record does not tell us what would have happened without the sign or rule out other changes. It establishes the decrease, not that the sign caused all of it. It also does not establish that the sign had no effect.

**Full-credit explanation anchor:** Recognize the decrease while explaining that other influences or a different no-sign outcome remain possible. Technical causal-inference terms or a specific comparison-group design are not required.

**Review concern:** All existing before/after causal examples have the same answer category. Review a larger bank for label cues and include supported descriptive claims before freezing forms.

## Decisions before a pretest

- Agree one language and accessible response format; a translated form needs its
  own review. The Chinese progress note is not a validated translation.
- Set one calculator policy and record it. Easy arithmetic can cause ceiling
  effects, while fractions and decimals can obscure evidence-use skills. Compare
  burden across these items; no difficulty level has been measured.
- Check that Supported / Contradicted / Not enough information are understood.
  A neutral familiarization example should not teach the scored task rules.
- Do not give item-by-item feedback during baseline. Common block-end feedback
  could itself teach the task families before practice: document this as shared
  preparation, not a pure untreated starting condition for later performance.
- Counterbalance order only after the full bank and feedback wording settle.
  Do not tell learners that exactly two answers occur in each category, and
  inspect whether repeated labels or wording invite guessing.
- Review B04's missing denominators for overly obvious cues and B06 for reflexive
  skepticism. Other forms need supported descriptive changes and sufficient-
  information cases; no comparison group is a universal requirement for every
  causal identification strategy.
- Recheck session burden before using 6 / 12 / 8 / 4 items. The 45–60 minute
  first-session estimate has not been established by testing.
