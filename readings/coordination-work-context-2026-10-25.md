# Work handoffs and shared understanding

**Reading note October 25, 2026 · AI-assisted source work; human review pending**

[Bechky (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.14.3.312.15162) reports a year-long
ethnography of one equipment manufacturer. Selected methods and findings were
read; this is not a replication or an AI study.

In the pump-fixture episode, installation order and space for assemblers' hands
were missing from the engineer's understanding. Translation did not settle the
issue; demonstration led to joint measurement (pp. 321–324, Table 2.1).
Engineers also used drawings in their own communication (p. 318), limiting a blanket claim
that documents fail. The discussion's examples of demonstrations failing are
proposed limits, not independently observed comparison cases (pp. 326–327).

The [episode record](work-handoff-example-2026-10-25.json) separates reported
actions from unreported approval, elapsed time and durable results. It is an
index into a published account, not a new dataset.

## A question to examine

When does a shared representation give different roles enough context to take
the next action? For a future AI comparison, distinguish component identity,
sequence, physical constraints, uncertainty and decision ownership from the
speed of producing a description. These are proposed observation fields, not
mechanisms established by this reading.

Seek a contrasting case in which an improved written or digital representation
does suffice. That would challenge a claim that physical co-presence is always
needed. Where parties understand one another but disagree over aims or bear
different costs, an explanation based on missing context may also be inadequate.
Neither contrast has been established here as an independent empirical case.
