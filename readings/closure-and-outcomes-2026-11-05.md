# A closed record and a resolved problem

**Bounded reading and observation proposal · November 5, 2026**

This unit changes the proposed observation, rather than adding another claim
about a better organizational structure. The question is what evidence lets us
distinguish the movement of a task from the completion of relevant work.

## Evidence and access

The [MIT draft by Pentland and Rueter](https://ccs.mit.edu/papers/CCSWP177.html)
was read through indexed primary-source text. Direct access timed out; neither
the final journal text nor original tickets were obtained. Section and figure
locators, evidence records and unknowns are in the
[record index](closure-and-outcome-records-2026-11-05.json) and
[source scope](sources-2026-11-05.json). This is not a full-article review.

The draft distinguishes record endings, tentative repairs and customer
confirmation. Its authors discuss closing without solving, reopening after
closure, and apparent cessation without a recorded ending. Figure 5 supplies
a coded handoff-and-return example; the displayed string does not reveal its
repair result or elapsed time. These are source reports, not new observations
by this project. No rates are recalculated from the reported departures.

## What changes in this project

The [November 3 reading](preparation-and-assignment-2026-11-03.md) separated
preparation, assignment and dispatch. This reading adds a different boundary:
an administrative state, the next action someone is expected to perform, and
evidence about the outcome need separate entries.

A candidate question is: when work moves to someone else for information or a
test, who takes it up, what permits it to return or stop, and how is the result
established? This has not been adopted as a final research question.

The six proposed observation fields and four **fictional** checks in the index
make this distinction inspectable. Two records can both say closed while only
one contains a relevant confirmation. Conversely, an open record may represent
a test still pending at the observation cutoff. Neither condition alone proves
good or bad coordination. A documented decision to stop may be appropriate;
the relevant purpose and evidence have to be stated.

One practical rule follows for the proposed reading sheet: do not infer
acceptance from assignment, elapsed delay from event order, or technical
resolution from an ending code. Record each as unknown if the evidence is
missing. Later reopening should remain visible rather than being erased by
the initial ending. These are proposals for review, not validated measures.

A separate [evidence-access design](../studies/organizational-coordination/evidence-access-pilot/README.md)
is now present on main. Its single-reviewer interface comparison cannot answer
whether work was transferred downstream; that still requires linked recipient
and rework evidence. This observation proposal is a companion question, not
an implementation or replacement of that design.

## Remaining test

Obtain a permissible linked record with an actual next-action request, evidence
of uptake or a documented obstacle, and a relevant outcome or observation
cutoff. Include an alternative such as an appropriate stop or a successful
handoff; do not assume fewer transfers or faster closure is always better.
The strict approved-but-stalled comparison remains unresolved. The indexed
draft also needs checking against a directly readable version before stronger
source claims are made.

AI assisted retrieval, interpretation, writing and structural checks.
Researcher understanding, independent source review and methodological review
remain pending. No original field data, general causal mechanism, AI effect or
change to the preserved learning-study protocols is claimed.
