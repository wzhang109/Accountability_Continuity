# Judgment feedback and responsibility in the AC project

**Current clarification · October 1, 2026**

The broad concern remains how people form judgment, encounter the consequences of decisions, and use experience when work is increasingly delegated to AI. This note makes the organizational branch measurable without treating every element of that concern as the same variable.

## Separate the objects of study

| Object | A concrete question | Evidence that is insufficient by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Substantive review | Did someone examine the relevant evidence? | Opening a file or signing a form |
| Practical authority | Could someone change or pause a decision without an inappropriate penalty? | The frequency of disagreement |
| Responsibility | Who is expected to explain the decision and respond to its consequences? | A human's presence at the last step |
| Outcome feedback | Who learns what happened after the decision? | A reporting channel existing on paper |
| Individual learning | Does experience affect that person's later performance? | The organization releasing a new version |
| Organizational learning | Is an experience retained and used beyond the original case or person? | One employee editing one answer |

These are proposed distinctions. The case pilot does not yet measure all of them.

## What improvement means

The [reflection on purposes](../../docs/purpose-judgment-and-accountability.md)
asks who defines success and whether experience can prompt revision of that
definition. Future evidence collection should distinguish changes to a process
under an existing aim from changes to the aim or measure itself. The current
entries do not establish either the legitimacy of an organization's goals or
whose perspectives were excluded. Missing evidence remains unknown; this
conceptual extension does not retrospectively turn the pilot into a test of values.

## Revisions to earlier claims

1. **Override frequency is not a discretion score.** The existing simulation already shows that different review and accuracy processes can produce the same frequency. Actual authority, sanctions, and interventions require separate evidence. The old 0–2 rubric is not a validated index and is not used in the pilot.
2. **Automatic execution does not define the disappearance of accountability.** Delegation changes who performs a step. It does not establish who retains duties to explain, monitor, remedy, or authorize changes. This is a conceptual distinction, not a legal conclusion about a case.
3. **One person and one role are not interchangeable.** A person can learn through repeated experience; a role can retain records when its occupant changes. Both are research objects. Neither is presumed necessary or sufficient for every form of accountability.
4. **A hard constraint is a normative modeling choice.** Requiring accountability does not logically establish that one specified continuity measure must always be non-negotiable. Earlier essays preserve that proposal as part of the project's development; it is not an empirical result.
5. **The branches answer different questions.** The simulation concerns measurement; the unimplemented advice-timing study concerns later task performance; this document pilot concerns observable organizational responses. None alone validates the full philosophy.

## Relationship to existing work

Raji and colleagues' 2020 paper proposes internal algorithmic auditing throughout system development, with documentation connecting stages. Its abstract was checked for this update, not its full framework. This makes clear that organizational AI accountability and documentation already have a research history. No claim of inventing the topic or closing a proven literature gap is warranted. [Original abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00973)

NIST's AI RMF Playbook provides voluntary guidance organized around Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage. It is a useful reference for situating operational questions, not evidence that a particular organization follows its guidance or that the pilot's measures are valid. [Official overview](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/nist-ai-rmf-playbook)

The candidate contribution is narrower: make a particular chain of evidence inspectable, preserve missing links, and eventually examine whether different response arrangements change subsequent decisions or learning. Establishing novelty and effects requires considerably more literature work and data.

## How the next empirical step can help

A usable record would link a reported problem to a response owner, the decision taken, implementation or a documented reason not to proceed, and later assessment. It should also show whether relevant people received the result. This could support a descriptive process study before any causal comparison is attempted.

A reasonable response need not always be a system change. Some issues should be resolved locally; some requested changes should be declined. The question is whether the response is justified, traceable, and connected to subsequent experience, rather than whether every complaint produces an update.
