# Proposed youth feasibility protocol

**Version 0.3 October 12, 2026 · proposed and not preregistered**

## Question and status

Compare initial judgment first with advice first on delayed performance without
advice. The no-AI arm provides a separately reported benchmark. This draft follows
the adolescent proposal submitted in October; submission is not funding or
approval. There is no agreed institutional partner, ethics clearance, participant
roster or original adolescent outcome data.

Recruitment must wait for a suitable adolescent-research partner, applicable
ethics review, parental permission and youth assent arrangements. Site, language,
accessibility, data responsibilities and compensation remain to be agreed. A
single setting is proposed; no cross-country effect is estimated. Participation
would be voluntary and separate from school grading.

## Population and planning counts

The first age band is 15–17. Up to 12 adolescents would help pretest wording,
difficulty, advice plausibility, effort and feedback. A different planned sample
of 90, provisionally 30 per arm, would enter the randomized pilot. These are
planning targets rather than a justified confirmatory power calculation.

Pretest feedback should inform revisions before registration and randomized
recruitment. Pretest and randomized cohorts must not be pooled as though they
received the same settled intervention.

## Tasks and learning target

Use fictional school-project records with an explicit claim, simple table and
bounded answer rule. Learners choose Supported, Contradicted or Not enough
information and give a short reason. Example skills are checking declared
constraints, choosing a denominator, pooling groups and distinguishing an
observed change from a causal claim. The full bank and comparable forms do not
exist yet. Sixteen examples support review: six baseline, six practice and four
delayed. Fourteen further items and reviewed forms are still needed. The
[rates practice review](PRACTICE_RATES_REVIEW.md) groups Y02, Y09 and Y10
with one key per answer category; that is not advice-quality balance. These are
not ready to administer. The separate [baseline guide](BASELINE_REVIEW.md)
documents starting-score interpretation, burden and feedback reactivity.

The learning target is evidence use on these rules. Arithmetic or language
ability might dominate performance; pretesting and specialist review must check
this. The task does not measure the quality of a person's life, broad agency,
moral responsibility or organizational learning.

## Workflow

| Phase | Planned items | Advice and feedback |
|---|---:|---|
| Baseline | 6 | No advice for anyone; no item feedback during the block |
| Practice | 12 | Assigned workflow; common verified feedback after each final response |
| Delayed assessment | 8 new related items | No advice for any original arm; record the entire block before feedback |
| Later challenge | 4 | Advice for the two original assisted arms; original no-AI arm remains unaided; common corrective feedback after each item |

A reproducible allocation procedure would assign participants after baseline.
The first session is provisionally 45–60 minutes. The second is approximately
seven days later, provisionally 20–30 minutes. Timing and counts need burden
checks; there is no new immediate unaided test added to this youth version.

The submitted proposal did not settle baseline feedback timing. This draft
proposes identical block-end feedback, after baseline answers are fixed. The
baseline policy and delayed block-end feedback are explicit clarifications for
review before registration, not descriptions of completed procedures.

For each practice trial:

- **Judgment first:** show the task; record first answer, confidence and reason;
  then show prepared advice; record a final answer, confidence and reason.
- **Advice first:** show the task with the same prepared advice; record a first
  answer, confidence and reason; permit review without new evidence or advice;
  record the final response.
- **No AI:** show only the task; record the first response; permit review without
  new evidence; record the final response.

The evidence table stays visible to everyone. Review screens must not disclose
an answer key. Time limits and question wording should be specified consistently;
equal instructions do not guarantee equal cognitive effort. Record actual burden.
The first answer in advice first is post-advice and is not an unaided baseline.

## Advice preparation and counterbalancing

Correct and misleading advice are scripted development materials, not measured
outputs from a deployed chatbot. Only one version would be shown per assisted
trial. An educator should verify keys and identify a plausible reasoning error
in the misleading version. Fluency, length and format should be checked without
claiming those checks establish equal psychological difficulty.

Use two complementary practice lists: each assigned learner would receive six
correct and six misleading items, with each item appearing in both conditions
across learners within each assisted arm. Challenge lists would similarly have
two correct and two misleading items. The full lists cannot be built from the existing six practice and four delayed
examples; the six baseline items have no advice and cannot fill practice slots. Learners must never see both versions of the same item.

The same item-level advice mapping and feedback policy apply in both assisted
arms. The no-AI arm has no advice-quality category; it must not be labelled as
receiving correct or misleading advice. Exact counterbalancing and item order
remain design decisions to register, not completed randomization.

## Outcomes and analysis

The primary measure is accuracy on the eight new delayed unaided items. The
primary contrast is judgment first versus advice first, adjusted for baseline
performance according to a reviewed analysis plan. Report estimates and
uncertainty; absence of a clear difference does not establish equivalence.

Record responses under original randomized assignment. Missing follow-up
outcomes remain missing; they are not coded as zero and cannot support a full
population intention-to-treat estimate without additional missingness assumptions.
Item nonresponse, technical interruptions, withdrawals and partial assessments
need prespecified rules before recruitment. The scoring helper intentionally
leaves an incomplete stage summary missing rather than inventing a primary score.

Secondary measures concern assisted performance, correct versus misleading
advice, final-answer confidence, explanations, effort and timing. In the later
challenge the assisted arms share current advice conditions. Comparing them
with no AI there combines prior workflow and current assistance; it is not a
clean common-interface comparison. Pre-advice correction opportunities exist
only in judgment first and are descriptive, not an equivalent measure across arms.

Two raters unaware of arm would assess short reasons with a piloted 0–2 rubric.
Record their separate scores before adjudication. Child-development and
statistical review remain pending. No automated score establishes independent
reasoning or validates the rubric.

## Feasibility and care

A provisional 80% follow-up completion goal is a management target, not a validated
threshold. Report recruitment pace, completion, burden, understanding of feedback,
floor/ceiling effects, rater agreement and reasons for missingness by arm.

The consent and assent process must explain appropriately that advice may be
inaccurate, with the detail and debriefing plan agreed through ethics review.
Use low-risk invented school records, common corrective feedback and the right
to pause or withdraw. Do not present medical, personal or financial advice or
let activity scores affect grades. The local helper is for researcher review;
it does not recruit, contact or collect participant data.

## Interpretation and contribution

The comparison tests the assigned workflow including order, effort and initial
commitment. One setting, near-transfer tasks and about one week cannot establish
lifelong effects. Prepared advice is narrower than live conversation. An initial
judgment could help checking or entrench an error; early advice could orient
attention or encourage unexamined agreement. Either benefit, cost or an uncertain
result is possible.

Related designs already exist. The focused contribution to investigate is the
feasibility of adolescent tasks with common verified feedback and a delayed
unaided primary comparison, not invention of judgment-before-advice. See
[SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). Public organizational authority and issue-response
records are a separate research branch.
