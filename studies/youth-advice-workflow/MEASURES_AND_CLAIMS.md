# Measures and the claims they can support

**October 10, 2026 · provisional specification**

| Measure | Stage and rule | Can support | Cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline accuracy | Six new starting examples, all unaided before allocation and feedback | Descriptive starting performance; possible prespecified covariate after review | A validated ability trait, reliable family subscales, or a causal learning estimate from raw pre/post change |
| Delayed accuracy | Eight new related items; all arms unaided; primary judgment-first versus advice-first comparison | Performance on the selected rules about one week later | General agency, lifelong development, pure timing effect or organizational accountability |
| Practice final accuracy | Same keyed rules after assigned workflow, before common feedback | Current task performance under that workflow | Independent learning from a correct assisted answer |
| Correct-advice use | Assisted items with verified correct advice; final keyed answer reported separately | Appropriate agreement on the selected task | Deep understanding from agreement alone |
| Misleading-advice response | Assisted items with misleading advice; jointly inspect keyed correctness and explanation | Susceptibility to the prepared error and ability to justify a correction | Good judgment from disagreement alone; unknown causal mechanism |
| First-to-final correction | First response versus final response; stages labelled by advice exposure | Descriptive correction and persistence patterns | Comparable unaided correction across arms; advice-first's first response is already assisted |
| Final confidence | Probability from 0–100 that the final answer is correct, recorded before feedback; compare with that answer | Task confidence and descriptive calibration/Brier error | Validated self-esteem, broad agency or evidence that confidence is understanding |
| Explanation quality | Separate answer accuracy; two blinded raters; draft 0–2 rubric | Correct use of decisive evidence and a stated rule in a short explanation | Internal cognitive independence from an articulate answer; validated reasoning scale |
| Effort and elapsed time | Self-report plus task durations and interruptions, with consistent stage labels | Acceptability and burden of the actual workflow | Psychological mechanism or deliberate thought from duration alone |
| Ownership and questionability | Exploratory post-session items, piloted wording | Reported experience of being able to explain, question or own a response | A validated general agency score or mental-health outcome |
| Completion and follow-up | Original assignment, stage denominators and reasons missing | Feasibility, with a provisional 80% management goal | Statistical power or success of a workflow from completion alone |

## Draft explanation rubric

0: no relevant reason, unsupported repetition or irrelevant evidence.
1: relevant fact or rule identified, but the decisive connection is incomplete or wrong.
2: the decisive evidence is connected to a valid rule supporting the answer.

Answer and explanation must be scored separately. Preserve both raters' original
scores and any adjudication. The rubric is proposed, not validated. A wrong answer
with sophisticated wording is not independent judgment; a short correct reason
should not be penalized merely for vocabulary.

## Confidence calculation

For a completed final response, let p be confidence/100 and y be keyed correctness
(1 or 0). A descriptive Brier error is (p-y)^2. Missing answers or confidence yield
no Brier value. Keep stages separate: first-response and final-response confidence
cannot be merged when their advice exposure differs. Group averages alone do not
establish individual calibration or a psychological mechanism.

## Missing assessments

Store missing follow-ups, unanswered items and technical interruptions explicitly.
The helper requires the expected number of answered items for a stage summary;
it does not silently change the denominator or impute zero. A statistician and
partner must settle partial-assessment and sensitivity rules before the pilot.
This conservative development convention is not a final missing-data analysis.


## Constraint explanation anchors

For Y11, a relevant reason connects the known budget failure to the irrelevance
of the missing seats, then compares the eligible plans. For Y12, it connects
unknown cost to eligibility and the possibility of different winners. Repeating
that a value is missing is insufficient on its own. Numerical scenarios are
optional; the rubric and rater agreement still need human review.
