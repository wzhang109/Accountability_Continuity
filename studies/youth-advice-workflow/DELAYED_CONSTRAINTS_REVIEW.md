## Keys and answer bases

| Y15, poetry event | A's seats | Not enough information | If A has 10 seats C wins; if A has 22 seats A wins. At 18 seats A wins the lower-cost tie. These are hypothetical completions, not observations. |

Y15 needs a link from uncertainty to a possible change in the claim. Merely
saying a number is missing does not establish this. Y16 needs both the exclusion
of C and the comparison between A and B. A short valid explanation is sufficient;
numerical examples are optional. Score answer and explanation separately using
the provisional 0–2 rubric, with independent raters and original scores preserved.
These anchors are not a validated scale or a second human review.

## Exposure, feedback and scoring

- No original arm receives advice, an advice-quality label, an answer key or
  coaching during a first/review assessment screen.
- Feedback remains withheld until the entire delayed block is complete and
  final outcomes are fixed. A single completed item is insufficient.
- The local helper's block-complete flag is a caller assertion, not an
  implementation that verifies eight actual responses. It is not a secure
  participant application.
- The primary measure still requires eight distinct delayed items. Six drafts
  do not justify replacing its denominator with six or using practice items.
  Missing follow-up remains missing, not zero.
- Block-end feedback may itself affect the later challenge. Its position
  relative to that challenge and the debrief needs to be fixed during protocol
  review. Delayed outcomes must be fixed first, and challenge differences must
  not be described as a clean measure of earlier learning alone.

## What this development step does and does not resolve

Both items contain an unknown value, yet the keys differ. Y16 also shows that
irrelevant missingness need not imply Supported. That is a logical check against
particular shortcuts, not evidence that learners use the intended reasoning.
Within this pair the missing field still predicts the label, and row/target
positions and repeated rule structure can cue answers. Difficulty and salience
are not matched. The tie rule is operative in a compatible Y15 completion but
not in Y16; rule burden may differ.

The six delayed drafts currently contain three constraint items and one each
for rates, pooling and causal evidence. Two keys fall in each answer category,
but that descriptive balance does not settle family weights, an eight-item
form, reliability, allocation or item order. Repeated tasks can teach strategies
within the assessment, even without feedback. No claim of far transfer, lasting
agency or improved real-world judgment follows.

Before any use, a researcher and educator should independently answer the
worksheet, check the evidence and wording, and review age, language, accessibility
and time burden. Child-research, ethics and statistical decisions remain pending.
